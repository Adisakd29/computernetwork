## Checked and found correct (focus topics)

- **Topology (w1 s5, quiz 5-7 and 10):**
  - Bus needs terminators, and the backbone is a single point of failure.
  - A basic single Ring breaks when any one link fails. FDDI dual ring is described as the exception.
  - In Star, the central device is the single point of failure, and one bad cable affects only one PC.
  - Mesh links = n(n-1)/2: 4 nodes give 6 links and 6 nodes give 15. Each node needs n-1 ports.
  - Hybrid/Extended Star (Tree) example is correct.
  - Quiz 7 (Bus disadvantages) key [0,1,3] is correct. "ใช้สายมากที่สุด" is false for Bus.
- **Protocols (w3 s3):**
  - TCP/IP is routable and NetBEUI is non-routable. IPX/SPX belongs to Novell NetWare and AppleTalk to Apple.
  - TCP is reliable (ACK and retransmit) and UDP has no ACK.
  - Ports: HTTP 80, HTTPS 443, DNS 53, DHCP 67/68.
  - The DHCP -> DNS -> TCP 443 -> HTTPS order in the coffee-shop example is correct.
- **OSI/TCP-IP (w3 s2, s4):**
  - Layer order and functions, and the mnemonic, are correct.
  - PDUs: Data, Segment/Datagram, Packet, Frame, Bit.
  - Mapping: Application = OSI 5-7, Transport = 4, Internet = 3, Network Access = 1-2. The 5-layer variant is mentioned. ARP placement is hedged correctly.
  - Quiz 3 and quiz 8 keys are correct.
- **IPv4 addressing, classes and subnetting numbers (w3 s5, quiz 5, 7, 10):** all recomputed.
  - Class A 1-126 /8: 2^24-2 = 16,777,214 hosts ("ราว 16 ล้าน").
  - Class B 128-191 /16: 2^16-2 = 65,534.
  - Class C 192-223 /24: 2^8-2 = 254.
  - Class D 224-239 is multicast and class E 240-255 is reserved. 127 is loopback.
  - Private ranges are 10/8, 172.16/12 (172.16.0.0-172.31.255.255) and 192.168/16. APIPA is 169.254/16.
  - 172.20.5.14 is class B and private. 172.32.1.1 and 8.8.8.8 are public.
  - Network and broadcast addresses 192.168.10.0 and .255 are correct for /24.
  - In the lab example, .1 is the gateway and .10-.50 are hosts, all inside 192.168.10.0/24.
  - The text answers '255.255.255.0', '/24' and '24' all grade correctly with the server normaliser.
- **Standards bodies and IEEE 802 (w3 s1, s6):**
  - ISO -> OSI; ITU (UN); ANSI -> FDDI; EIA/TIA -> TIA-568 T568A/B.
  - 802.1Q/1X, 802.2 LLC, 802.3, 802.5, 802.11 (CSMA/CA), 802.15.1 Bluetooth, 802.15.4 Zigbee, 802.16 WiMAX.
  - The "802 = Feb 1980" story is already worded as "มักเล่ากันว่า", which is appropriate.
- **w2 devices and media:**
  - Device-to-OSI-layer table is correct, and MAC addresses are 48 bits.
  - 10BASE5 is 500 m and 10BASE2 is 185 m (rounded to 200).
  - UTP and STP are both limited to 100 m.
  - Cat6 runs 10G to about 55 m, and Cat6a runs 10G to 100 m.
  - Fiber cores: SMF about 9 um; MMF 50/62.5 um.
  - GEO altitude is about 36,000 km (35,786 km).
  - Bluetooth uses 2.4 GHz with a range of about 10 m.
  - Infrared has a higher frequency than radio, which is correct.
  - Connector names (FC/ST/SC/LC) and SFP+LC usage are correct. Jacket colours are correct: SMF yellow, MMF orange/aqua.
- **w4 Ethernet standards:**
  - Standard numbers and years: 802.3 (1983), 802.3i 10BASE-T (1990), 802.3u (1995), 802.3z (1998), 802.3ab (1999), 802.3ah (2004), 802.3bz, 802.3an.
  - 100BASE-FX reaches 2 km full-duplex and 412 m half-duplex.
  - Gigabit ranges: SX 220-550 m, LX 5 km SMF (550 m MMF), CX 25 m, LX10/BX10 10 km.
  - BX10 uses 1310/1490 nm.
  - CSMA/CD gives up after 16 attempts, with binary exponential backoff.
  - Token Ring is 4/16 Mbps with an MAU. FDDI is 100 Mbps dual ring from ANSI.
  - SME example: 2 GB at about 11.5 MB/s is about 3 min, and at Gigabit about 18-30 s. Both are correct.
- **w5 cabling:**
  - T568A/T568B colours for all 8 pins are correct in the table, quiz and summary.
  - MDI pins: TX on 1,2 and RX on 3,6.
  - Crossover tester pattern 1-3, 2-6, 3-1, 6-2 is correct.
  - Pin-1 orientation is correct: contacts up, clip down, cable toward you, so pin 1 is on the left.
  - Untwist limit is 13 mm or less and bend radius is at least 4x the cable OD (TIA-568).
  - Channel is 100 m: 90 m permanent link plus 10 m of patch cords.
  - Router to PC uses a crossover cable (both ends are MDI).
- **w6 Wi-Fi:**
  - Bands and speeds for b/a/g/n/ac/ax/be are correct, and the speeds are labelled as PHY maximums.
  - Wi-Fi Alliance numbered names started in 2018.
  - 6E adds 6 GHz. Wi-Fi 7 uses 2.4/5/6 GHz with 320 MHz channels.
  - 2.4 GHz has 3 non-overlapping channels (1, 6, 11).
  - OFDMA and TWT belong to ax. MU-MIMO started in ac.
  - The NBTC (กสทช.) power-limit warning is appropriate.

## Unsure / not changed

- w4 s1 says FDDI "ครอบคลุมระยะได้หลายสิบกิโลเมตร". The standard allows a ring of up to about 100 km (200 km of fibre for the dual ring), so "หลายสิบ" understates it, but it is not wrong. Left as is.
- w3 s3 credits NetBEUI to "IBM และ Microsoft". IBM created NetBIOS/NetBEUI (1985) and Microsoft extended it, so the wording is acceptable. Left as is.
- w1 s4 sets the Peer-to-Peer size at "ประมาณไม่เกิน 10 เครื่อง". This is a common teaching guideline, not a standard. It is worded as approximate, so left as is.
- w3 already has 6 check blocks, one more than the spec's 3-5. This was existing structure, so no check was removed. The lead may want to merge one.
